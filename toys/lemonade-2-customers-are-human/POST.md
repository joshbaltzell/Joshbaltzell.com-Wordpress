Title: Lemonade 2: Customers are human
Excerpt: A game. The same lemonade stand, but now the customers notice .99 prices, remember what they paid last time, and see a $9 cup on the menu board.
Toy type: Game
Tags: game

---

This is the same 30-day stand as the first lesson, with the same bot beside you. The difference is the customers. Three findings from pricing research are built in, and each one can be turned off.

Charm prices. $2.99 starts with a 2, so it feels nearer $2 than $3. The menu now includes $2.99, $3.99, $4.99 and $5.99, and here a .99 price feels about 8 cents cheaper than it is.

Reference prices. Customers remember the last three days. A price above that feels like a loss, and losses weigh more than gains, so a raise costs more buyers than an equal cut wins back. Holding a price steady avoids the penalty. Jumping around pays it often.

Anchoring. The menu board shows a Grand cup at $9.00. Nobody buys it. Next to it, a regular cup looks modest.

At the end, the toy replays your exact prices on the same days to customers who only see the number, and tells you the difference, and which effect made most of it.

The sizes here are illustrative and kept small. The studies found these effects in specific settings, and they won't hold the same way at every stand.
