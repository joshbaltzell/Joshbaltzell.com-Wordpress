Title: Lemonade 4: Two competitors
Excerpt: A game. Two competitors, each with a simple price rule that reacts to you and to the other competitor. Some pairs of rules settle down. Others spiral.
Toy type: Game
Tags: game

---

Now there are two competitors. Each follows a visible rule, and each rule looks at the cheapest price among the other sellers, which includes the other competitor as well as you. By default one stays 25 cents under the cheapest and the other matches the cheapest.

Hold a steady price and watch what happens anyway. The first competitor cuts under the second. The second matches. The first cuts again. Within a couple of weeks both are at the lowest price their rules allow, and neither rule ever looked at a customer. The toy notes it on the chart when it happens, in a plain line such as "both competitors are taking turns undercutting each other."

Other pairs behave differently. A competitor that holds $3.50 keeps prices steady. Two competitors that stay 10% above the cheapest only climb if you climb too.

Visitors pick one of the three sellers, then buy or walk on. About one in seven are regulars at each seller, and the rest lean toward the cheapest. You can play by hand or write a rule, one day, a week, or to day 90 at a time.

A bot plays the same 90 days against its own copies of both competitors. It uses Thompson sampling (William R. Thompson, 1933) and learns only from what sells.
