Title: Lemonade 4: Two competitors
Excerpt: A game. Two lemonade stands across the street, each with a simple rule that reacts to you and to the other stand. Some pairs of rules settle down. Others spiral.
Toy type: Game
Tags: game

---

Now there are two stands across the street. Each follows a visible rule, and each rule looks at the cheapest price among the other stands, which includes the other competitor as well as you. By default one stays 25 cents under the cheapest and the other matches the cheapest.

Hold a steady price and watch what happens anyway. The first stand cuts under the second. The second matches. The first cuts again. Within a couple of weeks both are at their floor, and neither rule ever looked at a customer. The toy notes it on the chart when it happens, in a plain line such as "both stands are taking turns undercutting each other."

Other pairs behave differently. A stand that holds $3.50 keeps the street steady. Two stands that stay 10% above the cheapest only climb if you climb too.

Visitors pick one of the three stands, then buy or walk on. About one in seven are regulars at each stand, and the rest lean toward the cheapest. You can play by hand or write a rule, one day, a week, or to day 90 at a time.

A bot plays the same 90 days against its own copies of both stands. It uses Thompson sampling (William R. Thompson, 1933) and learns only from what sells.
